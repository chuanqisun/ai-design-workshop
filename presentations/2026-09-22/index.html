Direct agency · my thesis

Shneiderman vs Maes · Interactions 1997
  • The classic HCI debate: Direct Manipulation (Ben Shneiderman — continuous representation, immediate feedback, user control) vs. Autonomous Agents (Pattie Maes — delegation, background automation).
  • Direct Agency synthesizes the two: retaining tactile, continuous control while wielding the power of generative agentic models.

Part 3 · New [old] ways to design

Generate
→ Evaluate
→ Curate

  • The core computational design loop: Generate → Evaluate → Curate.
  • Provides a repeatable method for pairing human creative intent with generative algorithmic engines.

Step 1 · Generate

Combinatorial

CryptoPunks
  • Combinatorial exploration (exemplified by CryptoPunks): exploring vast design spaces by combining discrete attributes and generative rules.
  • Broad divergence forces generative models outside their narrow default assumptions.

Quantity over quality.

The ceramics class: the group graded on volume produced the best pots.

Art & Fear
  • Bayles & Orland’s ceramics parable from Art & Fear: The group graded solely on quantity produced higher quality work because they learned iteratively through rapid output.
  • With generative AI, generation cost approaches zero—use volume and rapid variation to discover unexpected solutions.
Granola
  • "Granola grounding": Grounding models with raw, multi-modal personal data (handwritten notes, lecture audio) rather than relying on abstract prompts.
  • Anchors generation in authentic personal context, transforming generative synthesis into a structured learning artifact.

Algorithmic literature review · my thesis pipeline

  • Algorithmic literature review: systematically mapping research landscapes and generating annotated bibliographies.
  • Use AI to index, cross-reference, and surface unaddressed whitespace—not to bypass deep critical reading.

Step 2 · Evaluate

Osborn · Applied Imagination
  • The Double Diamond design framework (Guilford & Osborn): Alternating divergence (broad ideation) with convergence (rigorous evaluation and focus).
  • Having diverged in generation, evaluation provides the critical filter needed to refine concepts.

Step 2 · Evaluate · techniques

  1. Quantification design rubrics → scores → sort, rank, filter
  2. Semantic axis sorting embedding distance to polar attributes: practical ↔ speculative
  3. Interactive data visualization LLM‑generated views of large solution spaces
  4. Virtual prototyping Time Travel Radio · Tangible Media Group
Prism · thesephist Seeing with language models · thesephist
  • Evaluation techniques:
  • 1) Quantification: translating design rubrics into numerical scores to rank and filter candidates.
  • 2) Semantic distance: using embeddings to map ideas across conceptual spectra (e.g. practical vs. speculative).
  • 3) Virtual prototyping: building quick software simulations (e.g. Time Travel Radio) to validate interactions before physical fabrication.

Step 3 · Curate

curare

Latin: to care for.

Selection is intent. Selection is the human act.

  • Etymology: Curate originates from the Latin curare, meaning "to care for."
  • Curation is where personal judgment, taste, ethics, and lived experience guide the final design outcome.

Return to the cliffhanger · Is the designer present?

Chiang · Why AI Isn’t Going to Make Art Ingold · The Textility of Making
  • Ted Chiang (Why AI Isn’t Going to Make Art): Art is defined by thousands of intentional choices; automating generation bypasses those crucial micro-decisions.
  • Tim Ingold (The Textility of Making): Making is an ongoing dialogue with materials—you discover your intent through the process of making.
  • Synthesis: Curation is the ultimate creative act in computational design. While AI generates possibilities, human curation injects lived experience and intentional meaning.
